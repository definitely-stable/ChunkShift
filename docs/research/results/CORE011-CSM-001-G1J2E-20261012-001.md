## Actual complete-chunk byte-preservation result

Every chunk from *each direction* was searched using **all of its exact raw bytes** at every possible offset of the other DLL using byte-for-byte comparison. This is not based on the 256-byte J2D source sampling grid. A whole-byte match forming an opposite canonical CSM chunk of the same length would additionally require equal `ChunkId`. Such a match would contradict the original public Plan result and fail the research test, not be silently accepted.

Frozen independent J2D base window oracles were reproduced without drift: **4 KiB** 2,069 exact matched base anchors / 798,464 B base union; **16 KiB** 634 / 291,328 B; **64 KiB** 275 / 135,680 B. For target-side partial coverage a **separate reverse scan** was performed with target-as-base anchoring every 256 bytes; its 803,072 B is not falsely inferred from base anchors.

Original CSM chunk size distribution: base minimum **20,150 B**, median **86,624.5 B**, maximum **212,846 B**; target minimum **17,997 B**, median **73,291 B**, maximum **239,690 B**. More than half of chunks are at least **64 KiB** in each version (54 base, 54 target).

A particularly important observed case is **base chunk index 60**, length **163,180 bytes**, with **162,365 bytes** covered by source 4-KiB windows that match somewhere in target (**99.50% sampled-window union coverage**). Nevertheless **its complete 163,180-byte source sequence is absent anywhere in the target DLL**. Thus positive near-whole coverage does not prove complete exact chunk preservation: the missing 815 B of certified *coverage* cannot simply be equated to changed bytes, and confirmed windows may map to different target locations. Conversely base chunk index 72, length 128,456 B, has 108,232 B match coverage; the source-to-target full-chunk search still fails.

The full-scanner results settle a narrower causal fork than the earlier J2D observation: **the reason no original canonical chunk could be reused whole is that no entire original canonical chunk survives unmodified anywhere in the opposite binary**. This is a complete-byte content constraint, **not** a discovered byte-for-byte-preserved entire chunk missed solely because its new offset or other-file CDC boundaries shifted.

## Product and mathematical interpretation

- Exact overlapping byte windows across versions **do exist**, even though entire canonical FastCDC chunks do not.
- With original chunk boundaries (74/81) held fixed, **no existing ChunkId can be reused without fetching/encoding different full bytes**.
- This does **not** establish the optimality or inadequacy of FastCDC. **A different, preregistered chunking profile or a lossless/reversible .NET-aware transform may preserve some unchanged subranges as complete chunks**. Proving potential gain requires constructing and verifying such exact candidate partitions plus patch-size/CPU tradeoffs; the present artifact gives neither.
- J2E cannot justify changing `CsmReader`, public `ChunkPatch.PlanAsync`, CSM/CSP wire, defaults, public API, or any NuGet package. It is not the requested representative >4096 chunk multi-CBLK workload, nor a product performance decision.
