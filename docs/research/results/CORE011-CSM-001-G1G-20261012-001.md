new allocations for repeated large blocks *after warmup*, while
retaining arrays beyond a call.

### Retention trade-off

After timed rounds and forced GC, source-free verification's managed
heap (bytes; the raw ZIP has the independent corresponding last-GC
LOH statistic):

| Verify workload | Eager | Lazy | Pool4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 MiB, 1 worker | 406104 | 398704 | **662472** |
| 384 MiB, 1 worker | 584456 | 580200 | **848240** |
| 384 MiB, 4 workers | 600352 | 607528 | **1645480** |
| 384 MiB, 16 workers | 610432 | 602088 | **1660688** |

Thus `pool4` at workers=16 retained **1,050,256 more managed bytes**
than `eager` after GC (~1.00 MiB higher), despite reducing ~143KiB
new allocations per verify call and eliminating Gen2 during timed
rounds. At small/1worker, pool4 retained ~256 KiB more than eager.
This is consistent with the intended bounded bucket retention,
but it is **not direct ownership or size attribution** of all objects.
The measured 384MiB case's process RSS still reflects the earlier
fixture construction and need not shrink after the content was
collected; it is not a peak CSM reader memory figure.

## Wall/CPU results are not yet an acceptance gate

On the same single hosted run, 384MiB/16-worker median batch wall was
**63.30ms eager, 82.29ms lazy and 60.61ms pool4**.
The 4-worker wall values were **16.26ms eager, 23.47ms lazy, 15.37ms pool4**.
In these lanes, lazy wall increased despite unchanged allocated
bytes/call and often lower aggregate process CPU time. This
disagreement exposes shared host scheduling and sequential
strategy-order confounding; **do not present these as proven
CPU regressions or improvements**. No dedicated affinity, A/B/A,
randomized order, independent repeated-run validation or
allocation-stack attribution is established here.

The Plan small-size batch medians (1 MiB: 0.773/0.733/0.697ms;
8 MiB: 2.293/2.208/2.212ms) are too short, similarly confounded and
are **not** a production wall-time decision.

## Decision and next slice G1-H

This experiment **PASSes the controlled correctness/managed-allocation
candidate feasibility gate**. It **does not ADOPT** any candidate.

1. For short CSM/Plan-heavy usage, `lazy` offers most allocation
   reduction **without process-global pool retention**.
2. For repeated large manifests, `pool4` offers major LOH/Gen2
   reduction but increases idle retained heap; bounded retention
   and clear-on-return matter.
3. Therefore the next candidate should be a **hybrid**:
   *start without a large array*, use an exact-size owned SOH buffer
   for CBLKs below the LOH threshold, and rent from a **bounded**
   pool only for large records; release in a `finally`-safe reader
   Dispose. Avoid allocating or warming a large pool on short-only
   readers. Pre-exposure CRC/semantic validation remains identical.

Before *any* production promotion: independently verify actual rented
bucket capacities, corrupt/truncated stream and cancellation cases,
forced-GC heap and physical RSS, concurrent 1/4/16/64, JIT and AOT
eligibility, and **randomized repeated A/B/A product Plan wall/CPU**
across short, large and real patch corpora on hosted runners.
Stale pooled contents must never be written and the pool must not
cross reader ownership boundaries. A product Amdahl **CPU claim**
still requires in-path attribution, not subtracting unrelated
benchmarks.
