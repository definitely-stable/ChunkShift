## Source-free fixtures and exact failure boundary

Baseline G1-J1 builder produced a physical CSM and deleted the full source content *before* any consumer process. Consumers used identical baseline-generated physical CSM bytes and the originally recorded ManifestId and FileDigest.

| Case | Logical source | Chunks | CBLKs | Physical CSM SHA256 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| BLAKE3 BIDX-on short control | 1 MiB | 11 | 1 | `472b7fcdb8c8c5dd28db9d0e54938824deb862f1153a9bc1de410a30a02c89bb` |
| BLAKE3 BIDX-on multi-CBLK | 384 MiB | **5052** | **2** | `342ff5e8175304be3f27873fdfeee051deaa0d3697d60e1dcdc3799e674eac2a` |
| SHA-256 BIDX-off multi-CBLK | 384 MiB | **5052** | **2** | `cb93b8bac6e1bd34fc5e72f7082d6296ea815f1c8a148f9cf0929af2f4a689b6` |

All **24/24** fresh-process verify/enumerate scenarios passed full public identity checks; **72/72** measurement rounds valid. `ManifestReader` enumerated every expected logical chunk with contiguous offsets using 31-entry buffers, even across CBLK boundaries. Every variant additionally corrupted the second CBLK *without correcting its CRC*; public `VerifyManifestAsync` and public `ManifestReader` both reported the same `BlockCrc`-containing failure flags and **only the 4096 first-block entries were exposed**. When the first block was corrupted in short control, **zero entries** were exposed. This proves these specific early-exposure/error predicates, not every error/cancellation lifecycle condition.

The 384-MiB experiment shows promising descriptive timing reductions in all four multi-CBLK lanes; comparing stage256 against the midpoint of the first and repeat baseline **only as a heuristic**, elapsed-time differences are roughly **−5.9%** BLAKE3 Verify, **−8.2%** BLAKE3 enumerate, **−22.9%** SHA256 Verify and **−31.9%** SHA256 enumerate. SHA256 enumeration baseline drift is particularly large (2.515→1.877 ms); the midpoint is **not** an unbiased estimator of the treatment effect. These are not confidence bounds, isolated hash-time shares, CPU acceptance or a claim of cross-platform superiority.
