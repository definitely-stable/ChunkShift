## Frozen strategies, direct pool capacity

- `eager`: current full 147500-byte buffer at reader construction.
- `hybrid1`, `hybrid2`, `hybrid4`: same G1-H validated
  80KiB-small-exact / bounded-pool-large reader. **The sole
  difference** between these three is
  `ArrayPool<byte>.Create(147500, maxArraysPerBucket=1|2|4)`.
  Small-only readers do not initialize the static large pool.
  Large arrays are cleared on Dispose; full CRC32C, hash, physical
  manifest, chunk and CSP validation remained unmodified.
- Pinned original `CsmReader.cs` SHA256
  `a62772638c715005cb8a1a6ca58222349755a7e21a341ff80655a825910cebf2`,
  fail-closed if source layout changes. Every variant SHA and
  common immutable checkout recorded in `build.json`.
- Frozen `order.json` specifies deterministic staggered variant
  orders; replicate1 reverses ordering. This reduces one
  sequential-test bias, but does **not** remove hosted CPU variability
  or constitute fully randomized independent repetitions.

The independent .NET direct probe `ArrayPool<byte>.Create(147500,cap)`
measured **262144-element arrays** for **all** cap=1/2/4 on both
runners:

| Max returned arrays per bucket | Actual array bytes | Theoretical retained bucket payload |

| 1 | 262,144 | 256 KiB |

These limits are for the **one large bucket's cached payload**,
not hard process RSS ceilings or direct reader-pool lifetime ownership.

chunk/plan oracles match across all variants and hosts.

| Scenario | Eager r0/r1 | Hybrid cap1 r0/r1 | Cap2 r0/r1 | Cap4 r0/r1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plan 1MiB w1 | 387.25 / 387.25 | **100.67 / 100.67** | **100.67 / 100.67** | **100.67 / 100.67** |
| Plan 8MiB w1 | 401.94 / 401.94 | **120.98 / 120.98** | **120.98 / 120.98** | **120.98 / 120.98** |

Short Plan/verify has effectively no capacity sensitivity,
confirming small-only operation doesn't allocate or warm the
large private pool. Large single-worker workloads likewise
reuse one returned large buffer. At worker4/16, lower cache
retention caps induce additional pool misses/new LOH arrays:
**cap1 still saves ~76–81%** allocation in the 16-worker
verification lane, **cap2 saves ~84–85%**, and **cap4 ~86.5%**
relative to eager under these two hosts.

This is **measured process-level GC**, not a guarantee of no

### Post-full-GC retained managed heap

These numbers are **whole-process managed heap** after source-free
verify, in KiB, not precisely the pool's private object graph
or total RSS:

| 384MiB verification | Eager r0/r1 | Cap1 r0/r1 | Cap2 r0/r1 | Cap4 r0/r1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| workers=1 | 573.7 / 569.6 | 828.0 / 831.1 | 831.2 / 831.2 | 825.3 / 831.5 |
| workers=4 | 585.1 / 585.1 | **842.5 / 838.4** | 1,098.6 / 1,091.5 | 1,603.7 / 1,606.8 |

At worker16, cap1 retains approximately **256 KiB** more
than eager, cap2 approximately **512 KiB** more, and cap4
approximately **1,024 KiB** more. The measured trend matches
nominal returned-bucket payload increments, but object ownership
requires independent lifetime tracing. The retention plateau
is not a memory-leak certificate.

## CPU/wall performance uncertainty

On runner0, 1MiB Plan median wall was ~3.1–3.3ms per
eight-call batch across candidates; on runner1 ~0.8–0.9ms.
For 8MiB Plan, the corresponding medians were ~15.7–15.8ms
versus ~2.3–2.5ms. This **large between-host timing difference**
demonstrates why wall-time medians must be compared *within
each host*, never pooled or presented as causal end-to-end
CPU speedups or single-request p95.

For 384MiB/16-worker verify:
- runner0 batch median ms:
  `122.66 eager, 114.60 cap1, 112.32 cap2, 112.86 cap4`;
- runner1:
  `68.35 eager, 61.55 cap1, 69.41 cap2, 74.47 cap4`.

The ranking is **not invariant** across runners. Therefore
there is **no stable CPU-performance winner**, despite repeatable
allocation/retention and GC trends. Even matched batch CPU
cannot attribute exclusive reader time: source-free verification
validates full CSM entries, and Plan does additional hashing and
set membership work.

## Interpretation / next decision gate

**G1-I feasibility PASS for capped pooling trade-off, product
ADOPT remains NO.** The three caps lie on a genuine measured
allocation/retention frontier, not a universal best choice.

- **cap1:** minimum measured idle large-pool retention; increases
  misses, allocations and Gen2 at 4/16 workers.
- **cap2:** under these hosted workloads offers **~84–85% allocated
  bytes/call saving vs eager** at worker16, with about **512 KiB
  less post-GC retained heap than cap4** and one Gen2 over five
  batches. This is a **promising candidate for further study**,
  not proven product default.
- **cap4:** strongest reduction of repeated LOH/Gen2; retains
  roughly **1 MiB more** after high-concurrency use than eager.
  Its runtime advantage is not repeatable across the two hosts.

**Next G1-J prerequisites:** freeze objective-specific budgets for
peak/idle process memory, acceptable allocated bytes per product
Plan, CPU regressions, and GC pause distribution; obtain larger
representative changed-file corpora, repeated independent hosted
CPU runs, contention/idle trim, cancellation/Dispose/fault injection
and Windows/AOT qualification. A public production `CsmReader`
buffer policy must then be compared on that evidence in a
separate reviewed non-lab PR. No production change in G1-I.
